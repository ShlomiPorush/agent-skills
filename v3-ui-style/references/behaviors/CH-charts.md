# UI behavior rules (v3 style): Charts

Part of the `v3-ui-style` skill. Companion to `references/v3-design-principles.agents.md` (look, behavior principles, interaction rules, what not to copy). The skill's SKILL.md maps ID prefixes to area files. Paths are relative to the skill folder.

How to read:

- One bullet is one behavior with a stable ID (`DS-1`). A rule may name rules of other areas by ID; when your screen uses that part, read those rules too.
- **Pitfall** marks a behavior whose history shows a failed earlier attempt, a revert or a fixed bug. It says what went wrong and what fixed it. Do not skip it: it is the reason the behavior is shaped the way it is.
- `v3:` gives the v3 helper and `file:line` in the bundled code under `references/v3-code/`, a copy of the mailcow Logs Viewer frontend at commit `8caf6e4` (`ui.css` is under `assets/css/`). Start at the cited lines and widen to the whole function and what it calls when the bullet is not enough. The rules were first written against the earlier snapshot `deb075d`, then checked rule by rule against this code.
- Rules the interaction fixes changed (October 2026) start with what v3 does now and name its helpers; the part marked "Before the interaction fixes" describes the code before them. Where the two differ, the first part is the rule; details the first part does not cover still hold. A sentence that starts "Before the auto-refresh layer" describes older code in the same way: history, not the rule.
- The principles file's section "Interaction rules v3 follows since its interaction fixes" lists what those fixes added. Never copy anything listed there under "Do not copy".

## Charts

- **CH-1 Pick a period, then a bar to see its numbers.** Mail flow shows one of three periods, picked in a segmented control in its head (24 hours, 7 days, 30 days): 24 bars of an hour, 28 bars of six hours or 30 bars of a day, cut where the app's time zone starts an hour, a quarter of the day or a day (a day can be 23 or 25 hours; a zone off by 30 or 45 minutes has its edges where its own hours start), the last bar running up to now. Each bar is clean mail with spam stacked on it, height `value / max * 100%` with `max = max(1, ...)`, zero segments hidden (`i[style*="height: 0%"]`), the running bar faded (`.is-partial` at `opacity: .55`), legend Clean / Spam. The axis names every third hour (bold every sixth) on hourly bars, the start of each day on six-hour bars, every fifth day on daily bars (none within five of the end; every day up to 10 bars), and "Now" under a running last bar; the last label never overflows the chart's end (`text-align: end; direction: rtl`); bars and axis stay left to right in RTL (`direction: ltr`). The four figures (Messages, Blocked, Deferred, Auth Fails) cover the period, each with the period before it under it ("Previous: 1,890", the same number of bars right before; " (partial)" added when that period starts before the logs kept, FB-9); Messages counts each message once, by its first-seen time, in the bar of its earliest delivery, with "511 deliveries" as a second line. The title reads "Mail flow, last 24 hours" ("last 7 days", "last 30 days"), or names the span when zoomed or a bar is picked ("Mail flow, 21/10/2026, 14:00 to 15:00", ", so far" for the running bar). Bar hover `ink 6%` only with a pointer (`@media (hover: hover)`); tooltip "21/10/2026, 14:00 to 15:00: 15 messages, 12 clean, 3 spam. Click for its numbers" (", so far" added on the running bar). Clicking a bar picks it: the figures switch to that bar with the whole span under them ("24 hours: 1,890", "Shown: 1,890" when zoomed), the other bars fade (`opacity: .3`), and Clear appears beside View these messages (CH-2; in a narrow panel a × beside the title); the same bar again, or Clear, returns to the period. A picked bar survives refreshes while it is still in the data; a period switch or a zoom drops it. On phones the chart goes from 120px to 90px and only every sixth hour is named (minor labels `visibility: hidden`). In a narrow panel (`@container (max-width: 480px)`, a phone) the head is one row: the period picker sits beside the title in short words (24h, 7d, 30d); the title says "Mail flow", or a picked bar's or a zoom's span with its days written without the year (`mailFlowSpan(from, to, formatDayMonth)`, "21/10, 14:00 to 15:00", ", so far" kept); it may take two lines beside the picker and is never cut; with a bar picked, Clear is a `24px` × beside it; under `340px` the title is `14px` and the picker's buttons tighter (`padding-inline: 7px`). The chart then has no row of actions: the Messages figure stands for View these messages (CH-2). Data: `/api/stats/flow?period=24h|7d|30d`, plus `previous=true` for the period before. Zoom: CH-9; strip: CH-10; one size: FB-17. Keyboard: the period buttons and the bars are buttons with `aria-pressed`, the bars with a focus-visible ring; period control `role="group" aria-label="Period"`; chart `role="group" aria-label="Messages per hour"` ("per six hours", "per day"); axis `aria-hidden`; the title is `aria-live="polite"`. v3: `setDashFlowPeriod`, `pickMailFlowHour()`, `renderMailFlowChart`, `renderMailFlowStats`, `mailFlowSpan`, `.ui-flow-bars.has-pick`, `#dashboard-flow-clear`; app.js:2678-2745, 2980-3110; ui.css:1405-1424, 1377-1381, 1455-1472, 1485-1493, 2865-2867; index.html:608-619.
- **CH-2 From a picked bar to the messages of exactly its span.** "View these messages" opens the list with a custom range of exactly the picked bar, or, with no bar picked, of all the time shown (the period or the zoomed span), and every other filter cleared: from the start to its last microsecond (the server sends each bar's `end_inclusive` and the span's `to_inclusive`, its end minus one microsecond); the date picker shows those days and the label names the span ("21/10/2026, 14:00 to 15:00"), both in the app's time zone. The bar counts each message once, in the bar of its earliest delivery; the list shows every message with a delivery in the span, so it can hold more than the bar when a message also has an earlier delivery in another bar. The dashboard figure counts like the Messages page, with the deliveries as a second line ("511 deliveries"). View these messages is always in place, disabled only while the chart has no data. The Messages figure is a button that does the same at every width (`#dashboard-flow-messages`, `title` "Opens these messages. Each message counts once, ..."): an arrow beside its name, its number underlined (`2px` at ink 25%, full ink on hover with a pointer or on focus), disabled with View these messages; in a narrow panel it replaces the View these messages row (CH-1). The alert window opens Messages the same way (CH-5). On DMARC, each day column of "Messages per day" opens that day's reports (the TLS chart the TLS day); a report day has a small 90px "Other days" chart to jump to another day. On the all-domains chart the columns are `disabled` because there is no single domain. Keyboard: columns are buttons (CH-3). Pitfall: the dashboard counted every delivery as a message, so a picked hour showed 511 and its Messages list 283 (#344). A test now checks the dashboard figure against the Messages list total. v3: `openMessagesRange`, `openMailFlowHourMessages`, `#dashboard-flow-messages`; ui.css:1293, 1397-1404; index.html:613; app.js:3112-3147; dmarc.js:457-459, 1010.
- **CH-3 Chart columns are buttons with a tooltip on hover and focus.** Each chart column is a button carrying its label in `data-tip`; hovering (a tap on a touch screen too) or focusing it shows a dark label above it ("14/09: 1,204 passed, 3 failed"; TLS "encrypted" and "failed") via `::after { content: attr(data-tip); bottom: calc(100% + 6px); left: 50%; transform: translateX(-50%); z-index: 3 }`, and focus or a hovering pointer brightens it (`filter: brightness(1.15)`, activity charts 1.3); a disabled column has `cursor: default` and does not brighten. The charts read left to right in both directions (`direction: ltr`). DMARC daily bars stack passed (green) under failed (red) with a legend and only the first and last dates on the axis. Y top is rounded up to the next multiple of the power of 10 below the max, with 5 gridlines; heights 150 (home), 190 (domain), 120 (TLS), 90px (other days); column max-width 46px; empty "No reports in the last 30 days.". Reference lines and markers do not catch the pointer (`pointer-events: none`). Keyboard: each column is a `<button>` whose `aria-label` equals the tooltip, and keyboard focus shows the tooltip too. v3: ui.css:2865-2886, 2993-3009; dmarc.js:441-459.
- **CH-4 A sparkline opens its chart dialog.** Each Mailbox Stats row has a tiny `96 x 24px` SVG line of sent plus received messages over the period (area fill accent 18%, 1.5px stroke, `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`; a flat grey line when empty), with a zoom cursor and a frame on hover. A click opens a dialog with sent and received columns per day (per hour for Today), a tooltip per column ("Mon, 05/10: 12 sent, 4 received"; days and hours named in the app's time zone, an hour a clock change repeats shown twice), the Sent, Received, Failed and busiest-day figures, and an "Open its messages" button that closes the dialog and opens Messages filtered on the mailbox. Dialog y top = max rounded up to a multiple of 4 (minimum 4), 5 gridlines; empty "No activity in this range."; Failed red at 10% and above; busiest "-" without activity; subtitle "Full Name · Last 30 Days, aliases included". Keyboard: the sparkline is a `<button>` with `aria-label="Activity of <user>: N messages. Open the chart"`, SVG `aria-hidden`, focus-visible border; dialog `aria-labelledby` the title. v3: `mailboxSparkline`, `openMailboxActivity`; mailbox-stats.js:454-510; ui.css:1888-1895.
- **CH-5 Activity behind an alert: the usual rate and the alert window.** Show the baseline next to the anomaly, plus the context needed to judge it: a volume spike, an SMTP block by abuse protection or an auth failure burst has "Show activity", opening a window with the mailbox's sent mail (or the username's failed logins) per 15 minutes up to two days shown, per hour beyond, several hours a column when the baseline is longer than a week (an hour column starts where the app's zone shows a whole hour), columns inside the alert window in red, for a spike a dashed amber line for the usual rate labelled "usual: 0.42 an hour" (at least .6% from the bottom, scaled to each column's real length; a block shows its limit as a figure instead), a red marker "Alert 14:05", hover "21/10/2026, 14:00: 12 sent" (" in 45 minutes" added when a clock change or the end of the data makes a column shorter or longer; the date numeric, FW-2), all in the app's time zone and on the clock Time Format picks (FW-9). Y-axis top = `max(4, peak)` rounded up to a multiple of 4, grid at quarters; period control 24 hours / 2 days / N days (FS-14), each the chosen hours and the two after the alert, counted back from the end of the data; sending alerts (spike, block) open on the N-day baseline view, failed logins on 24 hours. The chart zooms in time (CH-9) down to an hour, snapped to its quarters (the end of the data is an edge too), with a strip under it (CH-10); a range's own span is no zoom, and a range button is pressed only while nothing is zoomed. A click on a column picks it: its count under the chart ("21/10/2026, 14:00 to 14:15: 12 sent", with Clear), the other columns fade. Above the chart four figures with the alarming ones in red ("The window: Unknown" when the alert's window is not known, and then no columns are marked); under it "Around the alert" from the window's start to an hour after with bars for recipient domains, outcomes ("What happened to them") and subjects, or addresses, countries and networks, and a short note on how to read the pattern. A footer (`#alert-activity-foot`) stays at the bottom while the content scrolls and shows once there is something in it (not while loading or after an error): Dismiss (title "Dismiss the alert", while the alert is not dismissed) and Open in Messages or Open in Security. Open in Messages filters by the mailbox and the alert's time (from its window before it to an hour after); with a column picked it reads "Open this time in Messages" and opens that column, to a microsecond before its end, or, for the last column cut where the data ends, to that exact moment. Subjects get `dir="auto"` with the full text in the tooltip; empty "Nothing in the logs around the alert. They may have been cleaned up since."; a span without counts "No counts kept for this time.". Keyboard: columns are buttons with `aria-pressed` and an `aria-label` equal to the tooltip; Escape closes the window (DS-18). Pitfall: an alert says that something happened, not whether it is spam from a hijacked mailbox or a legitimate mailing, so #387 added this view. A follow-up kept the window's figures visible and its usual-rate label clear of the alert marker. On a phone a long alert title pushed the Dismiss button around until #478 moved the actions into the footer. v3: `openAlertActivity`, `alertActivityColumns`, `alertActivityShow`, `openAlertActivityMessages`; alert-activity.js:21-52, 80-189, 279-406; ui.css:2975-3038; index.html:1595-1609.
- **CH-6 A flow diagram lays itself out for its width.** Measure in JS and choose the layout by the container width: the DMARC mail flow shows "Your domain", "Sent by" and "Reported by" (top 8 senders, top 8 receivers) joined by curved lines whose width is volume (group `max(3, share * 24)`, link `max(2, share * 24)`, stroke opacity .45, round caps) and color the sender's pass rate (node tones at 95% / 80%). Wide: three columns each centred vertically, node width `min(210, (w - 80) / 3)`, 10px gap, 26px header. Under 600px the columns become rows down the page (44px between rows, 8px between nodes) and column titles hide. Redraw on resize (debounce 150ms) and keep watching the size while hidden so it draws correctly when the screen gets wider; at 760px and below the whole card is hidden (MO-6). A node is a `<button>` only when it has an action (sender nodes open their sender page). Screen reader: SVG `aria-hidden`. v3: dmarc.js:476-556.
- **CH-7 Charts read the theme tokens.** Canvas charts read their colors from CSS tokens with `getComputedStyle` at render, so they match the light or dark theme: blocked sends per hour or day as red bars (`--ui-fail`) on hairline grid lines (`--ui-line`), text `--ui-muted`. Bar chart, no legend, no x grid; x ticks unrotated, `autoSkipPadding: 12`; integer y ticks from 0; bar radius 3; height 180px. Destroy the chart before each reload and make the content visible before drawing so the canvas can measure. v3: rate-limits.js:97-104, 297.

- **CH-9 Zoom a time chart by drag, wheel, pinch or buttons.** A bar chart over time (the Dashboard's Mail flow, the alert window) zooms in time through one shared helper. A drag across the bars of an unzoomed chart (mouse or one finger) marks a band (accent `14%` with `1px` accent edges) and zooms to the bars under it, if they make at least the shortest span; on a zoomed chart the same sideways drag moves it. Ctrl+wheel, and a trackpad pinch (which Chromium sends as a Ctrl wheel), zoom around the pointer by `exp(deltaY * unit * 0.004)` (`unit` 16 for lines, 400 for pages), applied `220ms` after the last wheel event. On a touch screen two fingers pinch, keeping the time under them and following their middle; two fingers down to one go on as a move with no jump. A move under `6px` stays a click, which picks the bar; a mostly vertical move is left to the page (`touch-action: pan-y`, `user-select: none`); the click that ends a gesture picks nothing; a cancelled pointer calls the gesture off and the chart returns as it was. During a pinch, move or wheel the bars stretch and slide (`translateX(x) scaleX(k)` from `0 0`) until the new span is drawn. In a phone sheet a touch that starts on a zoomable chart (`[data-zoom]`), or a second finger, never moves or closes the sheet until every finger is lifted. Buttons under the chart, in `role="group" aria-label="Zoom the chart"`: Earlier, Zoom out, Zoom in (title "Zoom in (or pinch, drag across bars, Ctrl+wheel)", "columns" in the alert window), Later and Reset zoom, each with the same `aria-label` and `title`; a button that cannot act is `disabled` (`opacity: .35`), and Reset zoom keeps its place hidden until something is zoomed. Zoom in keeps the middle half, Zoom out doubles, Earlier and Later move by half; on the Dashboard Zoom in, Earlier and Later step by whole bars. Limits: at least an hour, at most the period (the alert window: all its data), kept inside it; zoomed all the way out is no zoom. The Dashboard asks the server for a zoomed span in bars of an hour up to 48 hours, of six hours up to 10 days, else of a day; the server snaps it to the nearest bar edges, at least one bar, at most 62 days, never past now. The alert window zooms over the quarters it already has, snapped to them. A redraw keeps the keyboard's place: a bar by its start, a bar that is gone to the bar holding its time (else the nearest), a button by its name in its group (a hidden one takes no focus), else the picked bar, else the group's first button. Keyboard: the buttons zoom; the bars stay buttons that pick. v3: `uiTimeZoom(box, { bars, span, limits, preview, change, cancel })`, `uiTimeZoomStep`, `uiTimeZoomClamp`, `uiRedrawKeepingFocus`, `dashFlowStep`, `alertActivityStep`; utils.js:2008-2248, 2527-2545, 2572-2576; app.js:2725-2728, 2747-2791, 2952-2973, 3022-3057; alert-activity.js:181-186, 191-247, 355-384; ui.css:1379-1388, 1454.
- **CH-10 An overview strip under a zoomable chart.** Under a chart that zooms (CH-9) a strip shows the whole period (the alert window: all its data) in small bars, with a window over the span the chart shows; it reads left to right in any language (`direction: ltr`). Drag the window to move it, drag an edge to resize it, or press beside the window to center it there; a drag starts after `6px`, and a mostly vertical one is left to the page (`touch-action: pan-y`). The span changes when a drag ends, and keys pressed in a row change it once, `350ms` after the last. Every change snaps to the chart's own bars, whole hours of the app's zone on the Dashboard (so even 30 days zoom down to an hour) and quarters in the alert window, except that the end of the data is an edge too; it keeps at least the shortest span, and an edge pushed against that limit steps back in doubling steps until it lands on the edge before it on an hour or day grid. Hour edges are found as the server finds its bars, walking UTC in quarter hours to where the zone's hour or offset changes, so zones off by 30 or 45 minutes (Lord Howe, Kathmandu) snap where their bars are. A refresh while zoomed draws the strip and its limits again and keeps the zoom (moved inside the new limits, dropped when less than the shortest span is left); the part older than the retention is hatched in the strip too (FB-9). Strip `height: 30px`, `margin-top: 8px`, `border-radius: 7px`, on an ink 5% ground; bars muted at `opacity: .45`, at least 6% high; window `1.5px` accent border over accent 14%, cursor `grab`, `grabbing` while held; edges `10px` by `22px`, cursor `ew-resize`, with a finger-sized target (`inset: -10px -12px`). Keyboard / screen reader: the two edges are `role="slider"` with `tabindex="0"`, named "Start of the span shown" and "End of the span shown", with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax` in milliseconds and dependent bounds (the start can go up to the shortest span before the end, the end down to the shortest span after the start), and `aria-valuetext` the time ("21/10/2026, 14:00"); arrows move one bar of the strip (Left and Down earlier, Right and Up later), Page Up and Page Down a bigger step (six hours on 24 hours, a day on 7 days, a week on 30 days, a day in the alert window), Home and End to the ends; the window itself is `aria-hidden`; the strip is `role="group"` ("The whole period, with the span shown"; "All the data, with the span shown"); a redraw keeps the focus on the same edge. Pitfall: a load that failed under a gesture left the chart stuck; since #479 an answer that arrives or fails under a gesture or a strip drag is set aside and asked for again when the gesture ends or is called off, and an answer made stale by a period or zoom change is dropped. v3: `uiTimeOverview(box, { limits, span, snap, step, label, change, idle })`, `uiOverviewMove`, `uiOverviewEdge`, `uiOverviewCenter`, `uiNearestLocalHour`, `alertActivitySnap`, `alertActivityOverview`, `renderMailFlowOverview`; utils.js:2297-2494; app.js:2793-2801, 2835-2950; alert-activity.js:197-212, 249-271; ui.css:1434-1450.
- **CH-11 A trend line under a figure.** Mail storage, To review and Banned now show their last 7 days as a small line under the figure (`72` by `18`, a muted `1.5` stroke with round ends, the latest reading marked by an ink dot of radius `1.8`): hourly samples, six hours to a point (their average, at their mean time), so it reads as a line, not a comb, and the latest reading itself ends the line. Samples more than an hour and a half apart break the line, so an hour whose source did not answer is a gap, never a drop to zero; a sample with no neighbour is a dot (radius `1.2`); a flat series runs at mid height. Nothing is drawn until the samples span a day: the place stays (`height: 18px`), and every card keeps an empty trend row, so the cards keep one height. Words for screen readers and on hover say the first and the latest reading and when the latest was read: "Mail storage used, last 7 days: from 8% to 11%, as of 10/22/2026, 14:29" ("Addresses to review", "Addresses banned" for the other two). It is a picture inside its card, not a button: the card keeps its own action, and the line opens nothing (unlike the Mailbox Stats sparkline, CH-4). It loads with the Dashboard and again every 5 minutes while the Dashboard is open (on its own clock, not with Needs attention), and is drawn again whenever its card is drawn; the trends of a feature that is off are not asked for. Screen reader: the SVG is `role="img"` with that `aria-label` and a `<title>`. v3: `uiSparkline(points, { days, now, format, label })`, `paintDashboardTrends`; utils.js:2250-2295; app.js:3225-3254, 3317-3318, 3326; index.html:635-640; ui.css:1576-1583.
- **CH-12 A domain's DMARC pass rate per day as a calendar.** A domain's DMARC page shows one square per day over the reports kept (`GET /api/dmarc/domains/{domain}/calendar?days=N`, `days` 1 to 400, the DMARC retention by default), in columns of weeks from Monday (days down, weeks across), headed "Pass rate per day" with "last N days". A day's tone follows its pass rate (SPF or DKIM passing): `>= 98` ok, `>= 90` warn, else fail; its strength follows its mail, `0.35 + 0.65 * log(total + 1) / log(max + 1)` (max: the busiest day), on the fill only (`color-mix(in srgb, var(--tone) calc(var(--strength, 1) * 100%), transparent)`), while the border stays in the full color (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1.5px var(--tone)`). A day without reports is only a `1px` `--ui-line` border and does not open (`aria-disabled="true"`); when the calendar reaches further back than the retention, a day before it is hatched (`135deg` stripes of ink at `25%`, `2px` apart) and its label adds ", reports of this day may have been cleaned up". A day with reports opens that day's reports. Each day is a button of `24px` or more (`28px` with 16 weeks or fewer) with its square drawn `inset: 3px` inside; its `aria-label` and tooltip carry the date as the app writes dates (numeric day, month and year), the messages and the pass rate ("<date>: <N> messages, <rate> passed DMARC", or "<date>: no reports"). The legend reads "98% or more", "90% to 98%", "Under 90%", "No reports" and, when hatched days show, "Before the retention". Time runs left to right in either text direction (`direction: ltr`); when the calendar does not fit its card (a year on a phone) it scrolls sideways inside it (`overflow-x: auto; overscroll-behavior-x: contain`), starting at the newest weeks. Days are days in the app's time zone with the same bounds as the day's own report view, so a day's figure matches what opens. Keyboard: one Tab stop (roving tabindex, first on the newest day); Up and Down move by day, Left and Right by week, Home and End to the ends, Enter or Space opens the day; a click moves the stop too; the grid is `role="group"` with a label that names the keys; focus ring `2px` in `--ui-focus`. Pitfall: the first review of #482 found four problems: right to left, a second Tab stop, contrast and target size; #482 fixed them with time left to right in both directions, the stop moving on a click too, the border kept in the full color while only the fill fades, and each day a button of `24px` or more with its square inside. v3: `dmarcCalendarCard`, `dmarcCalendarKey`, `dmarcCalendarStop`, `dmarcCalendarToEnd`; dmarc.js:370-439, 604, 715, 724; ui.css:1584-1603.
